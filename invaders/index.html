ALCHEMY BLOCK INVADERS

twenty-six glyphs, and every one of them does something different

Every block on the board is one of the twenty-six glyphs of the art — four elements, three principles, seven planets, twelve zodiacal operations. None of them is a reskin. Each behaves the way its own symbolism says it should, and that is the whole game: if you know what the glyph means, you can predict what the block does.

Tin is the metal of Jupiter, and Jupiter is the principle of expansion — so the Jupiter block swells when you shoot it and shoves its neighbours aside. Gold is incorruptible, so nothing marks the Sun block but gold itself. Quicksilver is the volatile spirit, so Mercury will not be caught — it flees somewhere else on the board, and you must fix it with a spell before it can be struck.

Shots set off blocks, and blocks set off each other. A copper bell rings everything within three cells; each of those does its own thing; theirs do too. Chains of forty and more are ordinary.

START HERE — THE CABINET

The first level is not a level. It is an exhibition: all twenty-six blocks laid out in labelled bays, nothing descending, nothing able to kill you, unlimited azoth and every power-up free for the taking. Shoot everything. Cast everything. Read what comes up. Leave when you want to.
